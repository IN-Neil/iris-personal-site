Journey Phone Prototype

The browser patch from the first report is now a real diff, built for production and measured in headless Chrome and in the iOS 26.2 Simulator. The measurements overturn the first report's framing recommendation. They also refute three of its claims (see the corrections at the end of this section).

CameraChoose Journey compact, not the crop. Compact is closer to desktop proportions, keeps departure whole, and needs no pan track. Crop failed its own sweep: the boat left the frame 22 times and every example was clipped.
Shared, whichever cameraThese changes are needed with either camera: lvh measurement, portrait overlay rules, an example slot (compact currently hides the examples), links after the scene, and an accessible story transcript.
Reading limitThe pinned copy fits up to about the 125% model. At 150% and 200%, and for the ending on small phones, copy must leave the pinned frame and flow with the page.
Untested on a deviceSmoothness, memory, rotation, bfcache, VoiceOver, and real Safari page zoom.
Corrections to the first report
  • "The crop keeps desktop relationships by construction" is false. Adult height ÷ dock height is 1.58 at 1440×900, 2.80 in crop, 2.53 in compact and 5.50 in uncorrected wide at 393×852. Width-sized and height-sized sprites only agree at the desktop aspect ratio.
  • Landscape copy measurements were wrong. They were taken with a full-width column override active at every width. Re-measured with the real desktop column, chapter one still covers the boat. That finding holds.
  • The "8–15 ms frame" figures were not work time. They were intervals between animation frames on a 120 Hz display, in an Electron browser pane. I've withdrawn them and replaced them with the traces in item 7.
  • "120 elements per frame" was counted over 60 frames, not per frame. Counted per frame, it's 112–121 elements. The size was right; the method was wrong.
  • "125% zoom" was a smaller viewport (314×682), not Safari page zoom.
  • Two of the first report's phone drawings came from an earlier 1.6-aspect run that contaminated the 0.9 measurements.

4 · What the extra camera movement does

Two pans. One is too narrow at each end

  • 0–0.12, hold at 6%. Departure: figures and dock visible, boat at the right edge. boat clipped at 393×852
  • 0.12–0.19, pan to 14%. Follows the boat drifting left, as the zoom goes from 1 to 1.4.
  • 0.19–0.84, hold. All four chapters: boat, moon (chapter two), and the example slot.
  • 0.84–1, pan to 40–41%. The zoom returns to 1 and the lighthouse (at 74% of the scene) enters. boat out of frame near 0.92 at 393×852

  • measured Direction and speed: the backward and random-jump sweeps failed at exactly the same positions as the forward sweep. Framing is a pure function of progress, with no path-dependent state. A fast flick can skip frames but can't produce a different frame. On a real device, only the in-between frames during momentum remain untested.
  • measured Zoom: the pan is applied outside the zoomed camera, which scales around the boat, so zoom never moved the boat out. The boat failures come from the stop values alone. Examples clip because x = 0.19 is scaled away from the boat pivot by 1.4×, landing 5–8px left of the window edge.
  • untested candidate Departure hold ≈ 10.5%, arrival ≤ 38%, slot x ≈ 0.215. These would need a clean sweep before anyone relies on them. The narrower the phone's aspect ratio, the smaller the valid range for each stop. That fragility is the main argument against the crop.
5 · Text fit

How it was measured, and where it breaks

Method: each chapter was measured at 20% through its segment, when its panel is fully typed. For each panel I recorded the rendered rectangle, the boat rectangle, and whether the typed text had reached the full body length (true for all 48 chapter measurements). The ending has no typewriter. Heights include the complete body either way, because Typed reserves the full invisible copy.

Zoom was modelled, not applied: 125% = 314×682, 150% = 262×568 and 200% = 196×426. Each is 393×852 divided by the zoom factor, assuming Safari's aA zoom shrinks the CSS viewport. Smallest readable fallback without a nested scroll

Unpin the copy, not the scene. If a panel's measured height exceeds its band (from the top inset to the boat's top minus 16px), render that panel as a normal block inside the tall container at its segment's scroll offset. It then scrolls past on the page, over a scrim, while the scene stays pinned behind it. That keeps one page scroll, no inner scroll area, complete copy, and the boat still moving. For the ending, always put the links and footer in normal flow after the scene on phones. This is essentially the lead's second alternative, used only where the pinned layout doesn't fit. not built

6 · Viewport strategy

The large viewport, cached; re-measure when width or lvh changes

container height  = 30 × 100lvh          sticky scene = 100lvh
measure()         top = rect.top + scrollY, height = rect.height,
                  lvhPx = probe(100lvh).height, width = clientWidth
progress          = clamp((scrollY − top) / (height − lvhPx))      // no innerHeight
on resize         if width unchanged AND |probe lvh − lvhPx| < 1 → ignore
                  else measure(), scrollTo(top + kept × (height − lvhPx), "instant")
also              fonts.ready → measure; pageshow → measure

Simulator evidence: iPhone 17, iOS 26.2 Safari, default tab bar simulator, not device

The page scrolled to scrollY 690 at progress 0.0316. Only innerHeight, visualViewport and dvh changed, by 40px. The old formula divided by innerHeight, so it would have changed progress mid-scroll. The new one didn't re-measure. Screenshots of the readout are in this conversation, not attached.

Why skip these resizes, and which ones still count

  • Skip: toolbar collapse or expand. Nothing the formula uses changes, so re-measuring would only add layout reads and a chance of a jump. The rule is "width and lvh unchanged", not "only the height changed". The first report's wording was wrong.
  • Handled because lvh or width changes: resizing a desktop window's height, iPad Split View and Stage Manager, rotation, and page zoom (width in CSS px).
  • Not covered by this rule: content height changing without a resize (late fonts are handled; anything else isn't). Use a ResizeObserver on the container instead of relying on fonts.ready. recommendation
  • New issue found: with the toolbar showing, the bottom 40px of the 100lvh scene sits under the toolbar, and the route map (bottom-6) sits in that strip. Pinned controls should anchor to the small viewport, for example bottom: calc(100lvh − 100svh + 1.5rem). untested
  • First paint: the prototype chooses its framing after mount, so phones briefly render the wide framing first. Production needs the framing decided before first paint: CSS media queries and container units for sizes, instead of a JS prop. untested
  • untested Expanding the toolbar again, Bottom vs Compact tab layout, rotation (these Simulator tools can't rotate), the Back button (bfcache), and a physical phone.
7 · Performance evidence

A production build, measured in headless Chrome

Method: production static export; headless Chrome driven over the DevTools protocol; 393×852 mobile emulation. At each of four points (chapter 1, chapter 2 examples, storm, arrival), the page scrolled 60 steps of 12px, one per animation frame, with instant scrolling. Per frame, a MutationObserver counted style writes and distinct elements touched. A Chrome timeline trace was summed by category on the renderer process and divided by 60. I ran each point at unthrottled CPU (Apple silicon) and at 4× CPU throttle.

Two earlier runs were discarded because of harness bugs: smooth scrolling cancelled the scroll steps, and an empty observer callback swallowed the records. Headless Chrome paints and composites differently from a real GPU, and 4× throttling is not an A-series phone. 8 · Accessible story content

Removing JourneyStacked, as the prototype does, loses the story for screen readers

Measured from Chrome's accessibility tree at page load:

Two things hide the content. Stage is aria-hidden, which covers every floating example. panelStyle also sets visibility: hidden on each chapter and the ending until their scroll moment, and that removes them from the accessibility tree. Desktop has this problem today; phones don't, only because of JourneyStacked.

Proposal not built

  1. One transcript in story order. Place a visually hidden <article> before the sticky scene, built from the existing Panels: intro, then for each chapter its heading and body followed by its questions (QuestionPanel) or milestones (ChapterPanel showItems), then the ending. DOM order is the reading order: context before examples, exactly as JourneyStacked has it now.
  2. Hide the visual duplicates from assistive tech. Mark the sticky overlay group aria-hidden, keep Stage hidden, and give headings a single ID set.
  3. Links: milestone titles in the transcript are plain text. The one project link also appears in the ending navigation. That navigation is the only focusable set, and it reveals itself on :focus-visible so a sighted keyboard user never tabs into something invisible.
  4. Verify with the VoiceOver rotor (iOS and macOS) listing headings and links in order, and desktop Tab order.
Recommendation

The smallest implementation I'd build now

Compact camera, full-screen, plus the changes the evidence says either camera needs. No crop track, no crop.ts.

Evidence that would send me back to the crop

  • Compact with examples enabled fails the sweep, and no single slot clears its larger moon and clouds at both 393 and 320 widths.
  • Design review judges compact's 2.1–2.5 figure-to-dock ratio further from desktop than crop's 2.8. On numbers compact is closer, but this is a visual call.
  • The compact lighthouse can't be kept whole at arrival without its own pan. At that point the crop's pan track is no longer extra complexity.
  • A physical iPhone shows compact and crop with the same memory and frame cost, and designers prefer the crop's framing. Frame cost did match in headless Chrome; memory hasn't been measured.

Evidence that would send me to the shorter scene with normal-flow text

  • At 125% real Safari zoom, or on a 375-wide phone with the toolbar showing, chapter copy doesn't fit, so the unpinned fallback becomes the common path rather than the exception.
  • A physical older iPhone stays under 60fps through the storm even after the star change. In that case, reducing how much of the scene is pinned is the cheaper lever.
